01 — Recording & Remote Studio

Capture studio-quality audio, even on a guest's bad Wi-Fi.

The first bottleneck most podcasts hit is recording quality. Two failure modes: bad room acoustics (echo, reverb) and bad internet drops during remote interviews (choppy audio, lost sentences). The three tools below address both. Riverside records each track locally in up to 4K video, so a guest's Wi-Fi dying only loses the call — not the audio. The Shure MV7 is the durable USB mic that has become the de-facto standard for podcasters who record at home. Zoom is the no-surprises default for guests who don't want to download a new app.

The 2026 trend: every serious recording platform now ships AI-powered auto-leveling, noise reduction, and echo removal baked in. The mic is still the foundation, but a clean recording no longer requires a treated room.

★ Top pick · Remote

Riverside

Remote studio recording
The dominant remote recording studio for podcasters. Each guest's audio and video records locally on their device up to 4K, then uploads to the cloud — so a guest's Wi-Fi dropping only loses the call, not the audio. AI features include auto-transcription in 100+ languages, AI noise removal, magic clips (auto-generated short clips), and AI show notes. The standard for interview shows.

02 — Editing & Production

The transcript-as-edit workflow.

This is the highest-leverage category. Editing is the time bottleneck that decides whether a podcaster publishes weekly or monthly. The breakthrough of 2024-2026 is the "edit audio by editing text" model: tools like Descript transcribe your audio, let you delete filler words and silence by clicking on the transcript, then re-render the audio to match. The learning curve is shorter than traditional timeline-based editors (Audition, Pro Tools, Reaper), and the time savings are 4-10x.

If you only adopt one tool from this list, adopt Descript. The transcript-as-edit model is the single largest workflow improvement podcasting has seen since the DAW.

03 — Transcription & Captions

Searchable, quotable, indexable.

Transcription has gone from a luxury to table stakes. Google indexes podcast transcripts now, Apple Podcasts surfaces them, and Spotify displays them as live captions. A transcripted episode is a 3x more discoverable episode — both on the podcast platforms themselves and on Google search. Three tiers: included (in Descript and Riverside — most people don't need a separate tool), mid-tier (Otter.ai — best meeting-grade AI notetaker, also great for podcasts), and free/open (Whisper — local, private, requires more setup).

04 — Audio Enhancement & Cleanup

Make any recording sound like a studio.

The 2026 generation of audio cleanup tools is genuinely magic. A noisy cafe recording, a guest on a laptop mic in a reverberant kitchen, a podcast recorded on the road — feed it through Adobe Podcast Enhance or Krisp and the output sounds like a treated studio. The technology is diffusion-model denoising — the same family as Stable Diffusion and DALL-E, but applied to audio spectrograms. Three tools, ranked by accuracy vs. cost vs. processing speed.

06 — Hosting, Distribution & Monetization

Get the episode to every podcast app.

Hosting is the back-end that stores your audio files and feeds them to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, YouTube Music, Overcast, Pocket Casts, and every other podcast app. The good news: hosting is a mature market with three clear winners, and the pricing is similar across all of them. The bad news: every host now claims "AI-powered" something — most of which is just auto-generated show notes or transcripts you can do in Descript or Otter for free. Pick the host with the best distribution + the analytics you need.

Monetization is a separate layer. Three paths: (1) host-ads (Acast, Spotify Audience Network), (2) listener-support (Supercast, Memberful), (3) programmatic via your own ad sales. Most new podcasters start on host-ads and migrate to listener-support once they hit ~1,000 listeners per episode.

Questions podcasters actually ask

What is the single best AI tool a new podcaster should start with?

Start with Descript. It collapses the recording-edit-transcribe-show-notes workflow into one tool, and its "edit audio by editing text" model is the shortest learning curve in podcasting. Pair it with a USB mic (the Shure MV7 at $249 is the durable default) and either Riverside or Zoom for remote interviews. Skip the standalone AI transcription tools — Descript's transcription is good enough that adding Otter or Whisper is duplicative. Total starter stack: Descript Hobbyist $24/mo + a $100-$300 mic = under $350 to ship episode one.

Is Descript or Riverside the better pick for an interview show?

Different jobs. Riverside is the recording studio — local-record-each-track-up-to-4K-video means a bad internet drop on your guest's end doesn't ruin the audio. Descript is the editing studio — transcript-as-edit, filler-word removal, AI voices, studio sound, and clip generation. Most interview shows end up with both: record on Riverside, edit in Descript, distribute via Buzzsprout or Transistor. If you can only afford one and your show is solo, pick Descript. If your show is 80%+ interviews, pick Riverside first and add Descript when the editing backlog starts to hurt.

Can AI actually replace a human podcast editor?

AI can replace ~70% of the mechanical work of editing (filler-word removal, silence trimming, noise cleanup, transcript generation, show notes, chapters, social clips) but the remaining 30% is judgment — pacing, narrative arc, what to cut for story, what to leave for texture — that still wants a human. The honest framing: AI turns a 4-hour edit into 40 minutes of human review. The human still has to do the review. For shows with budget, the optimal is: AI does the mechanical pass, a human editor does the creative pass, the host does the final review.

How much does a real podcaster AI stack actually cost per month?

Realistic monthly stack for a serious podcaster in 2026: editing (Descript Pro, $33/mo) + remote recording (Riverside Standard, $24/mo) + hosting (Buzzsprout or Transistor, $12-$19/mo) + clip generation (Opus Clip or Castmagic, $19-$29/mo) + transcription overlap is included in Descript. Total: $88-$105/mo. Recovers 6-10 hours/week of editing and clip production. The economics flip positive when the recovered time supports one extra episode per week or one extra interview per week, which compounds audience growth. Free option: Descript Hobbyist at $0/mo with watermark, Audacity for editing, Riverside free tier (2 hours/mo recording), Buzzsprout free tier (2 hours/mo hosting) — works for the first 10 episodes.
